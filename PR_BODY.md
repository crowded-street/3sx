## `Game/com/shell` — ten files, 8.28–8.81 → **10.00**

body is the same switch on `CP_Index[wk->wu.id][0]` that `passive` and `active` are made
of. So it belongs against the same shared skeleton module, and `passive_fold.py` reaches
it with a new `FAMILY` entry.

Two commits:

- **`xfold`, new.** `gfold` only ever groups the scripts it is handed against each other,
  so a script that is one of a kind in its own folder stays inline even when its body is,
  character for character, a skeleton an earlier fold already produced. `xfold` matches a
  script against the *existing* skeletons and rewrites it as one call — **32 of 117 on the
  first pass**, all onto skeletons the passive and active passes had built.
- **`gfold --min-members 2`** takes the other 84 onto nine new skeletons, at no cost to the
  patterns folder (every one of its 14 scores unchanged).

## `Game/com/patterns` — plateaued at 8.02, and priced rather than guessed

The first plateau in this campaign argued from CodeScene's own published thresholds
instead of from a series of experiments. `rules_config_list_thresholds` gives
`file_mean_cyclomatic_complexity_warning` = 4 and
`function_duplication_min_lines_of_code_for_check` = 10, and both findings follow from
those two numbers and the shape of the idiom:

- A skeleton's complexity is its switch arms plus one, so a file of three-step skeletons
  sits at exactly 5. `tailsplit` (new — Recipe X's shared-tail variant, generated) splits
  105 skeletons across 22 shared tails and takes the folder mean 4.69 → 4.40. Still above
  4, **every one of the 14 scores unchanged**, reverted under rule 2. The arithmetic then
  says no variation can work: the folder's own mean is above the threshold, so no
  arrangement of those functions into files clears it.
- Recipe F over all 14 files folds 57 skeletons onto 25 and measures **8.02 → 8.02**. That
  retires the passive folder's risk-based refusal in favour of a measurement taken where
  it belongs.

Two levers that *would* move the number are written down and **refused**, because each
makes CodeScene stop looking rather than make the code better: re-bucketing the files to
balance the mean, and reformatting an arm onto one line to drop a skeleton under the
ten-line duplication check.

One correction: the note claiming by-step-count grouping is "the one grouping that can put
any file under the file mean threshold" is backwards — it puts the low-step files under and
guarantees the high-step files sit at the maximum their contents allow.

## Catalogue and tooling

- **Recipe V amended.** A named constant is a varying literal: it is written out verbatim
  at its own call site and the helper does nothing with it but the operation that was
  there, which is the whole of the recipe's safety argument. What stays excluded is a
  varying *expression* that reads state, calls, or could trap. Two narrower points with it
  — pass a computed mask whole rather than the bit it is built from, so the helper computes
  nothing from a parameter; and a varying struct field travels as its address, with two
  field types needing two helpers.
- **`tools/ch.py`, new.** `codehealth_sweep.py`'s transport aimed at the inner loop: hand
  it the files just changed, get their scores *or their findings* back in one server

- **`passive_fold.py xfold`** and **`tailsplit`**, described above; `step_map` learned to
  bind a tail helper's arguments so `verify` sees through it.

  reason the next recipe does not reach each one.
